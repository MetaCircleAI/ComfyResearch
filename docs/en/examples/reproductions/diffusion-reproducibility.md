:::{admonition} Abstract
:class: cr-abstract

This example probes the same-noise reproducibility phenomenon reported by
Zhang et al. Two CIFAR-10 DDPMs share their initialization, data subset, and
sampler noise; only training-seed randomness differs. Across data sizes, paired
samples move from unstable train-set memorization to fine-grained,
less-train-near agreement. This is a controlled qualitative reproduction, not
a replication of the paper's full architecture, seed, or dataset sweep.
:::

**Paper:** [The Emergence of Reproducibility and Generalizability in Diffusion
Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05264), Huijie Zhang, Jinfan Zhou, Yifu Lu,
Minzhe Guo, Peng Wang, Liyue Shen, and Qing Qu (ICML 2024)

**Template:** `repro: Diffusion same-init / different-seed`

**Template ID:** `repro-diffusion-same-init-different-seed`

## Reproduction Goal

The paper reports that independently trained diffusion models can produce
similar outputs when given the same starting noise and a deterministic sampler.
It interprets this as evidence that reproducibility can occur in both a
memorization regime and a generalization regime.

This graph asks a narrower question: when all conditions except training seed
are fixed, how do paired output agreement and proximity to the training set
change with the number of CIFAR-10 training examples? We compare the two
deterministic maps generated by the endpoints, rather than claiming that their
score functions are identical.

The template deliberately omits checkpoints and historical image assets. It is
an editable workflow for producing new endpoint pairs; the results below are
the reference runs from this controlled study.

## Experiment Configuration

| Item | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Dataset | CIFAR-10, class-balanced fixed subset, `subsetSeed = 0` |
| Data sizes | 512, 4,096, and 32,768 training images |
| Model | Small 32x32 UNet-DDPM, shared initialization seed 0 |
| Optimizer and loss | Adam, learning rate `2e-4`, diffusion MSE objective |
| Endpoint A / B | Same data and initialization; trainer seed 0 / 1 |
| Batch size | 256 |
| Training budgets | 5k, 10k, and 20k steps, depending on the comparison |
| Sampling | 64 shared initial-noise inputs and deterministic DDPM sampling |
| Observables | Paired similarity, RP/SSCD, and nearest-train GL |

`GL` is used here as a nearest-training-image proxy: higher values indicate
that generated samples are less similar to their nearest training examples.
It is not a distributional generalization guarantee.

## Run in Comfy Research

1. Open **Templates** and load `repro: Diffusion same-init / different-seed`.
2. Choose a `trainSize` and matching training-step budget. The editable default
   is the 4,096-image, 5k-step smoke configuration.
3. Keep the dataset subset, UNet initialization, optimizer, and sampler noise
   fixed. Set the two Trainer seeds to 0 and 1.
4. Train both endpoints, save or load their checkpoints, and run both
   deterministic samplers with the shared noise seed.
5. Run paired similarity and RP/SSCD. Run nearest-train GL for each sampler to
   distinguish paired agreement from simple training-image proximity.

## Results

At 20k steps, the data-size sweep shows a progression from unstable
memorization to fine-grained reproducibility.

| Train images | Paired MAE | LPIPS | RP | GL, seed 0 / seed 1 | Reading |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 512 | 0.135 | 0.124 | 0.402 | 0.000 / 0.000 | Unstable memorization |
| 4,096 | 0.081 | 0.083 | 0.976 | 0.032 / 0.087 | Coarse-reproducible transition |
| 32,768 | 0.053 | 0.044 | 1.000 | 0.414 / 0.379 | Generalized fine-grained reproducibility |

The five historical Comfy Research runs make the metric trends directly
inspectable. Each canvas contains the two endpoint workflows, deterministic
sample grids, paired similarity, RP/SSCD, and nearest-train comparisons.

The additional step sweep sharpens this picture:

- For 512 examples, longer training does not repair the paired map: RP falls
  from 0.497 at 5k to 0.402 at 20k, while GL remains near zero.
- For 4,096 examples, RP can be high before images are closely aligned: at 5k,
  RP is 0.998 while MAE is still 0.147. Feature-level similarity therefore
  saturates before fine-grained paired agreement.
- For 32,768 examples, the correspondence stabilizes early: at 10k, MAE is
  0.056 and RP is already 1.0, close to the 20k result.

## Interpretation

The results support three setting-specific observations.

1. **Memorization does not imply reproducibility.** With 512 examples, both
   endpoints overfit and generate train-near samples, but the same-noise
   assignment differs across training seeds. Continued training strengthens
   model-specific memorization rather than aligning a shared map.
2. **Reproducibility develops with data scale.** The 4,096-image regime is an
   intermediate state: coarse feature agreement is high, yet paired pixels and
   perceptual details still differ.
3. **Large-data reproducibility is consistent with generalization.** At 32,768
   images, paired MAE and LPIPS are lowest while GL is substantially higher and
   train/test losses are close. The agreement is therefore not explained only
   by nearest-training-image copying.

## Limitations

- The study compares two same-init, different-seed endpoints only; it does not
  test the paper's broader architecture or independent-initialization claims.
- The setting is CIFAR-10 with one DDPM and one deterministic sampler.
- RP/SSCD, MAE, LPIPS, and nearest-train GL measure different aspects of the
  phenomenon; none alone establishes full distributional equivalence.
- The observed trend is empirical evidence in this controlled setting, not a
  proof that the learned score functions coincide.
